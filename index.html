Find the size of ∠BAC

The equal lengths create three isosceles triangles. A straight-line angle then turns one expression into 2x, after which the final triangle contains the angles x, 2x, 2x.

Final answer: ∠BAC=36°
The most important reading detail is that A, D and C lie on one straight line. Therefore the angle BAC is the same angle as BAD.

Because D lies on AC, ∠BAD = ∠BAC =x.

1

AB = BC makes triangle ABC isosceles

In triangle ABC, the equal sides are AB and BC. The side opposite AB is AC's angle at C, while the side opposite BC is AC's angle at A.

Equal sides have equal opposite angles, so

∠BAC = ∠ACB.

The angle at A is x, therefore

∠ACB =x°.

Since D lies on the same straight line as A and C, ray CD points in the same direction as ray CA. Hence

∠BCD = ∠BCA =x°.
2

DA = DB makes triangle ADB isosceles

In triangle ADB, the equal sides DA and DB meet at D. Therefore AB is the base, and the two base angles at A and B are equal.

The angle at A is ∠DAB=x°, so

∠ABD =x°.

The three angles of triangle ADB total 180°, so its angle at D is

∠ADB = 180°−x°−x° ∠ADB =(180−2x)°.
3

The straight line turns the next angle into 2x

A, D and C lie on one straight line. Therefore the two adjacent angles ∠ADB and ∠BDC form a straight angle:

∠ADB + ∠BDC =180°.

Substitute ∠ADB = 180° − 2x:

(180−2x)° + ∠BDC =180°.

Therefore

∠BDC =2x°.

This is the exterior-angle theorem in another form: an exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles, here x + x = 2x.

4

BC = CD makes triangle BCD isosceles

In triangle BCD, the equal sides BC and CD meet at C. Therefore BD is the base and the two base angles at B and D are equal:

∠CBD = ∠BDC.

We have already proved that ∠BDC = 2x, so

∠CBD =2x°.

Add the angles of triangle BCD

The interior angles of any triangle total 180°. Therefore

2x +x +2x =180.

Collect the five x's:

5x=180.

Divide both sides by 5:

x = 1805 =36.

Hence

∠BAC =36°.

A second, shorter check using the angle at B

Triangle ADB gave ∠ABD=x, and triangle BCD gave ∠DBC=2x. Therefore

∠ABC =x+2x =3x.

But AB = BC makes triangle ABC isosceles, whose other two angles are x and x. Thus its angle at B is also

∠ABC =180−2x.

Equating the two descriptions gives

3x =180−2x ⇒5x=180 ⇒x=36°.

Numerical angle check

Triangle ADB

Its angles become 36°, 36° and 108°, which total 180°.

Straight line at D

108° + 72° = 180°, so ∠BDC = 72°.

Triangle BCD

Its angles become 72°, 36° and 72°, which total 180°.

Triangle ABC

Its angles become 36°, 108° and 36°, consistent with AB = BC.

Common mistakes to avoid

Mixing up the equal-side groups

AB, BC and CD form one equal group. DA and DB form a second equal group. The problem does not say all five segments are equal.

Putting equal angles at the wrong vertices

In an isosceles triangle, equal angles are opposite the equal sides, not necessarily where the equal sides meet.

Ignoring the straight line

A, D and C being collinear is what changes 180° − 2x into the adjacent angle 2x.

Using ∠BAC and ∠BAD as different angles

Rays AC and AD point along the same line from A, so those two angle names describe the same angle.

Final answer

The required angle is

∠BAC =36°.

The decisive final triangle is BCD, whose angles are 2x,x,2x. Their sum gives 5x = 180°.
